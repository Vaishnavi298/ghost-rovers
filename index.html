Ghost Rovers

NASA has left robots on the Moon, on Mars, and out in deep space. Some went quiet. Some are still talking. Scroll down and let four of them tell you their own stories.

Chapter 1 · The Moon

Eagle’s Descent Stage

Landed July 20, 196921 h 36 min on the MoonStatus: left behind
Science it unlocked

The crew set up a package called EASEP: a seismometer to listen for moonquakes and a dust detector, powered by solar panels so it only worked in daylight. It was switched off in August 1969. The laser mirror next to it needs no power at all. Its big sister, ALSEP, flew on later missions. Source: NASA NSSDCA.

Chapter 2 · Mars

Opportunity the Marathon Rover

Landed Jan 24, 2004Drove 45.16 kmLast contact June 10, 2018
Science it unlocked

By studying rocks, Spirit and Opportunity confirmed that water stayed on Mars’ surface for a long time in the ancient past. Spirit landed in Gusev Crater, a possible former lake, drove 7.7 km and finished its mission in 2010. Source: NASA Science.

Chapter 3 · Mars, again

Ingenuity the Little Helicopter

First flight Apr 19, 202172 flights · 128.8 minFlew 17 km total
Science it unlocked

Ingenuity scouted ahead for the Perseverance rover, photographed terrain from above, and even spotted the rover’s landing hardware. NASA’s planned SkyFall mission builds on its success with next-generation Mars helicopters. Source: NASA Science.

Chapter 4 · Interstellar space

Voyager 1, Still Talking

Launched 1977Interstellar since 2012One light-day: Nov 18, 2026
Science it unlocked

Voyager 1 and 2 were built to use a planet alignment that happens only once every 176 years. Voyager 2 entered interstellar space in 2018. Both still study space beyond the heliosphere, the Sun’s protective bubble. Source: NASA news release.
